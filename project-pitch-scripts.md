# RTI Online — Phase 1 & Phase 2 Video Pitches

## Project in one sentence

**RTI Online** makes India’s Right to Information process easier to understand and complete by turning a complex government workflow into a guided, accessible digital experience.

---

# Phase 1 — Product Walkthrough

## Goal

Show the problem, the overall product, and one complete user journey in about two minutes.

## Minute 1 — Problem and project overview

### Show

1. Home page
2. The four primary actions: Submit Request, View Status, Submit First Appeal, and Public Authorities
3. RTI Request Life Cycle / guided journey section

### Say

> Filing an RTI request should help citizens get public information, but the process can feel difficult before a person even begins. People may not know which public authority to choose, what information to write, whether they need to pay a fee, how to track the request, or when they can file an appeal.
>
> We built RTI Online as a clearer, modern citizen-facing experience for the RTI workflow. It brings together request filing, public-authority discovery, request tracking, first-appeal guidance, payment reconciliation, and accessible help in one place.
>
> Instead of treating the process as a long government form, the interface explains the journey in stages and guides users towards the right next action.

## Minute 2 — Main workflow in action

### Show

1. Select **Submit Request** from the home page
2. Accept the request guidelines
3. Open the request form
4. Search for a ministry / public authority
5. Enter a short RTI question and use **Rewrite with AI**
6. Submit the request and show the generated registration number
7. Open **RTI Request History** or **View Status** to show the saved request

### Say

> Here is the core journey. A citizen starts a request and first sees the guidelines, so they understand the rules before filing.
>
> In the form, they can search for the right public authority rather than being limited to a short list. They can write their question in plain language and use Rewrite with AI to make it clearer and more suitable for an RTI application.
>
> After submission, the user receives a registration number. The request is then visible in Request History and can be tracked through View Status. This makes the experience feel like one connected journey instead of separate, confusing pages.

## Phase 1 closing line

> Our aim is simple: make the right to ask for information feel understandable, guided, and accessible to every citizen.

# Phase 2 — Differentiating Feature Pitch

## Focus feature

**RTI Sahayak Voice Assistant** — a conversational, voice-led guide that helps a user move from a spoken problem to an RTI-ready draft and the correct filing journey.

## Minute 1 — Problem and product in action

### Show

1. Home page, including the clear RTI journey and service update strip
2. Language controls, then RTI Sahayak / Voice Assistant controls
3. Public Authorities Directory search
4. Request form and Request History very briefly

### Say

> In Phase 1, we built a connected RTI journey where citizens can find a public authority, file a request, track its status, and understand the First Appeal process.
>
> But our bigger problem is to make RTI filing simple enough for anyone to use, while helping them complete their task as quickly as possible.
>
> Today, a citizen may know the issue they face but not the department, authority, or formal wording needed for an RTI request.
>
> To solve this in Phase 2, we built RTI Sahayak. It uses natural-language AI to understand a citizen’s problem, recommend the relevant department and public authority, and prepare an editable RTI request draft. Personal profile details are filled only within the authenticated portal flow; they are not sent to the AI. The citizen reviews every detail before submitting.

## Minute 2 — RTI Sahayak Voice Assistant

### Show

1. Open the Voice Assistant and show its conversation appearing in the RTI Sahayak chat panel.
2. Say: **“Hey RTI, I want information about pension schemes for senior citizens.”**
3. Show the assistant identify or recommend the relevant authority and prepare the request direction.
4. Continue to the RTI form and show the editable draft plus profile-prefilled details.
5. Briefly point out that the user reviews the form before submitting; do not show real personal data.

### On-screen proof points

- The user begins by speaking naturally, not by selecting a department or learning form terminology.
- The spoken turn is visible in the accompanying RTI Sahayak chat, so the interaction remains understandable and reviewable.
- The assistant recommends; it does not make the final filing decision.
- Personal profile information is filled only after the authenticated handoff and is never sent to the AI.

### Say

> That is the RTI journey we created. But we wanted to take it one step further: what if a citizen could begin that journey before knowing the process, the department, or even the right words to type? This is RTI Sahayak Voice Assistant. A citizen can tap the microphone, say “Hey RTI,” and describe their issue naturally in the language they are comfortable with—English, Hindi, Telugu, or Kannada.
>
> RTI Sahayak uses natural-language AI to understand the concern, recommend a relevant department and public authority, and prepare an editable RTI draft. What makes our approach different from a normal chatbot is the handoff: a spoken problem becomes a structured filing journey.
>
> When the user continues to the request form, their saved profile details are filled securely by the portal—not shared with the AI. The citizen reviews the authority, draft, and personal details before submission, completes the mock payment and the request is created. So we make RTI filing more accessible and faster without taking away privacy or user control.

## Phase 2 closing line

> RTI Sahayak does not replace the citizen’s decision. It reduces the knowledge barrier, keeps the filing process understandable, and helps more people use their right to information with confidence.

# Recording checklist

- Keep each phase video under two minutes; rehearse once with a timer.
- Use a fresh demo account and predictable mock request data.
- Keep browser zoom at 100% and hide development tools, console output, and local URLs.
- Use one realistic example throughout: pension information for senior citizens.
- Do not claim that the prototype is an official government service or that AI provides legal advice.
- In Phase 2, spend most of the second minute on the voice interaction and the resulting filing handoff—not on every portal page.
